**Subject:** A year of working with AI on music

Dear Professor Doornbusch,

I hope you are well.

Last year I took your *Songwriting with Algorithms* class, and I bought a MIDI keyboard for
the final project. I told myself I would probably use it one day to make something of my own.
A year later that is still ahead of me, but the way I am approaching it has changed quite a
lot, and I thought you might be interested to see where it went.

The first thing I built was an MCP for Cardinal, the VCV Rack fork, so that an AI could
drive the synth directly — load a patch, turn a control, read back what was running. That
made it possible to sit down with an AI and work out a process together for getting a piece
of music I had in my head into Cardinal, and then building patches inside it with the AI in
the loop. Alongside that I made an extension for my keyboard: it records what I play and
turns it into something a sequencer can use, which is what lets me arrange rather than only
improvise. Recently we have been using that same workflow to try to reconstruct the music of
Quadeca, which has been a good way to find out what the process actually needs.

Everything is in one repository if you would like to look at it or use any of it:
github.com/Lijiahong77/cardinal-mcp

I should be honest that essentially all of it was written by the AI working with me. That is
stated at the top of the readme, and I think it should be. I have used it and I have made
the musical decisions, but the code is not something I wrote line by line.

I am starting postgraduate study at the University of Melbourne next year. I only found out
afterwards that you began there yourself, which I would have liked to know at the time.

I still have not written my own song. I still intend to, and if it happens I will send it to
you before anyone else.

Thank you for the class. It is still working on me.

With best regards,
Li Jiahong (John)
